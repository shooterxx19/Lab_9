Task 1: Estimate Housing Prices Near Transit
- Slope: -0.7262 (approx. -0.73 price units per 100 m)
- Intercept: 45.8514
- Predicted price at 500 m (distance_100m = 5): 42.22 (10,000 NTD/ping)
- Explanation: The negative slope (-0.73) shows an inverse relationship: moving further away from the nearest MRT station decreases housing prices. Each additional 100 m reduces the predicted price by about 0.73 units (7,300 New Taiwan dollars per ping).

Task 2: Compare Models for Yacht Resistance
- Linear Baseline 5-Fold CV RMSEs: [9.52, 9.69, 9.57, 7.64, 8.33] | Mean CV RMSE: 8.95
- Quadratic (Degree-2) 5-Fold CV RMSEs: [4.76, 4.44, 4.62, 3.31, 3.82] | Mean CV RMSE: 4.19
- Model Choice: The degree-2 polynomial model is significantly better because its mean CV RMSE (4.19) is more than 50% lower than the linear baseline (8.95), capturing the non-linear relationship between Froude number and residuary resistance.

Task 3: Tune a Concrete Strength Model
- Dataset split after duplicate removal: 804 training rows, 201 test rows
- Linear Baseline Mean CV RMSE: 10.20 MPa
- Top 3 Grid Search Settings:
  1. degree=3, alpha=1 -> Mean CV RMSE: 6.09 MPa
  2. degree=3, alpha=0.1 -> Mean CV RMSE: 6.13 MPa
  3. degree=3, alpha=10 -> Mean CV RMSE: 6.23 MPa
- Tuned Model Test Performance: Test RMSE = 6.52 MPa | Test MAE = 4.91 MPa
- Comparison: Tuning polynomial degree and Ridge regularization improved mean CV RMSE from 10.20 MPa down to 6.09 MPa, proving that non-linear terms and ingredient interactions substantially improve strength prediction.
- Example Degree-2 Interaction Term: Cement Water (cement multiplied by water).
- Why Scaling Belongs Inside the Pipeline:
  1. Prevents data leakage: StandardScaler computes mean and standard deviation strictly on the training folds during each CV split before transforming the validation fold.
  2. Fair Ridge penalty: Higher-degree polynomial terms have much larger numerical scales than linear terms; standardizing ensures Ridge shrinks all coefficients equally.
